## How to use this playbook

This playbook is for reviewing commercial non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) where our client is mainly the party receiving the other party's Confidential Information. The party providing information is the "Disclosing Party" or "Discloser". The party receiving information is the "Receiving Party" or "Recipient". Our client is treated as the Recipient unless stated otherwise. In a mutual NDA, each party acts as the Discloser when it shares information and as the Recipient when it receives information.

- **Preferred:** the position that best protects our client as the Recipient.
- **Fallback:** a compromise we may accept if the Discloser will not agree to the Preferred position.
- **Resist:** a term that creates unnecessary risk or burden for our client. Seek to have it changed. Escalate any unresolved Resist position to the supervising senior before agreeing to it.

Before reviewing, confirm which party is the Discloser and which is the Recipient under each relevant clause. Check that defined terms are used consistently. Read the entire NDA, including its definitions, schedules and standard clauses. Check for protections that are missing as well as wording that creates risk. Focus the mark-up on points that matter to the client.

## 1. Definition of Confidential Information

This clause defines the information the client must protect. The client should be able to identify which information is covered.

- **Preferred:** Confidential Information should cover information the Discloser provides to the Recipient after the NDA is signed, if it is marked confidential or, for an oral or visual disclosure, identified as confidential when shared. The Recipient's notes and analyses should be covered only to the extent they contain or reveal that Confidential Information.
- **Fallback:** Accept a definition that also covers information that is not marked, but only where its confidential nature would be clear to a reasonable person in the circumstances.
- **Resist:** All information the Discloser has ever shared with the Recipient, whether or not it is confidential, marked or reasonably identifiable. Also resist treating every Recipient note or analysis as confidential even where it does not reveal the Discloser's information.

*Tip:* The client's notes and analyses should only be treated as confidential to the extent they contain or reveal the other party's Confidential Information.

*Sample clause:* "Confidential Information" means non-public information disclosed by the Disclosing Party after the Effective Date that is marked as confidential or, if disclosed orally, identified as confidential at the time of disclosure.

## 2. Exclusions

The NDA should clearly exclude certain information from the definition of Confidential Information, such as information already known to the client or developed independently.

- **Preferred:** Include all four exclusions: information that (1) is or becomes public through no breach by the Recipient; (2) the Recipient already knew without a duty to keep it confidential; (3) the Recipient lawfully receives from a third party that is not under a confidentiality duty; or (4) the Recipient independently develops without using the Discloser's Confidential Information.
- **Fallback:** — (none)
- **Resist:** Any missing exclusion; a requirement to prove an exclusion by "clear and convincing evidence"; or wording that makes an exclusion ineffective merely because some related or general information is public or already known.

*Tip:* Check that the independent-development exclusion is included. It is sometimes missing.

*Sample clause:* Confidential Information does not include information that (a) is or becomes public through no breach by the Receiving Party; (b) was known to the Receiving Party before receipt; (c) is received from a third party without a duty of confidentiality; or (d) is independently developed by the Receiving Party without use of the Confidential Information.

## 3. Purpose and permitted use

This clause sets out what the Recipient is allowed to do with the Confidential Information. Ensure that the purpose is broad enough to cover everything the Recipient needs to do in connection with the proposed transaction or business relationship, including evaluation and negotiation.

- **Preferred:** Allow the Recipient to use the Discloser's Confidential Information to evaluate, negotiate and, if agreed, carry out the specific proposed transaction, relationship or project. Permit copying, sharing and internal analysis reasonably needed for that purpose.
- **Fallback:** Accept a narrower purpose if it still covers all steps the Recipient reasonably needs to take, including internal assessment, discussions with advisers, due diligence and negotiation.
- **Resist:** A purpose so narrow that it prevents the Recipient from carrying out its evaluation; a ban on making reasonable working copies; or restrictions on internal analysis needed for the agreed purpose. Do not accept a restriction that prevents the Recipient from using its own information or independently developed materials.

## 4. Permitted recipients

The client should be able to share Confidential Information with the people it needs to involve, without obtaining additional consent from the Discloser.

- **Preferred:** Allow the Recipient to share Confidential Information with its affiliates and its and their directors, officers, employees, contractors, consultants and professional advisers.
- **Fallback:** Accept a narrower list of permitted recipients, provided the Recipient can still share Confidential Information with its relevant affiliates, employees, contractors, consultants and professional advisers who need the information to evaluate, negotiate or carry out the proposed transaction or business relationship.
- **Resist:** Restricting disclosure to employees only; requiring the Discloser's consent before sharing information with affiliates or professional advisers; or requiring professional advisers to sign a separate NDA when they are already bound by professional duties.

## 5. Standard of care and security

The client should only have to meet a reasonable level of care to protect the information.

- **Preferred:** The Recipient must use reasonable care to protect the Discloser's Confidential Information against unauthorised access, use or disclosure.
- **Fallback:** The Recipient must protect the Discloser's Confidential Information using at least the same degree of care it uses to protect its own similar confidential information.
- **Resist:** "Highest degree" or absolute care; compliance with security policies that the Discloser can change unilaterally; strict liability for any security incident; or an unreasonably short fixed notice deadline. Resist obligations that require the Recipient to guarantee that no security incident will occur.

## 6. Compelled disclosure

This clause allows the client to disclose information when the law, a court or a regulator requires it.

- **Preferred:** The Recipient may disclose Confidential Information where disclosure is required by applicable law, regulation, court order or a governmental or regulatory authority, without obtaining the Discloser's consent or being required to challenge the disclosure.
- **Fallback:** Where permitted by law and reasonably practicable, the Recipient must notify the Discloser before disclosing the Confidential Information to the relevant court, governmental authority or regulator.
- **Resist:** Any requirement for the Recipient to obtain the Discloser's consent before making a legally required disclosure; to use best efforts to resist or challenge the disclosure; to seek a protective order at its own expense; or to obtain a legal opinion before complying. Also resist wording that could delay or prevent a disclosure required by law.

*Sample clause:* If the Receiving Party is required by applicable law, regulation, court order or governmental or regulatory authority to disclose any Confidential Information, it may make such disclosure without obtaining the Disclosing Party's consent. Where legally permitted and reasonably practicable, the Receiving Party will notify the Disclosing Party before making the disclosure.

## 7. Term and survival

The NDA should set a clear end date for confidentiality obligations.

- **Preferred:** Confidentiality obligations should continue for 1 year from the date on which the relevant Confidential Information is disclosed to the Recipient.
- **Fallback:** Accept a confidentiality period of up to 3 years from the date of disclosure.
- **Resist:** Any indefinite or perpetual confidentiality obligation; any confidentiality period exceeding 3 years; or wording that restarts the confidentiality period each time information is accessed, copied or used.

## 8. Return and destruction

The client may not be able to remove information from routine backups and may be legally required to keep certain records.

- **Preferred:** At the Recipient's choice, allow it to return or destroy the Discloser's Confidential Information within 30 days after receiving a written request or termination of discussions. The Recipient may retain copies required by law or regulation and routine back-up copies that cannot reasonably be deleted.
- **Fallback:** — (none)
- **Resist:** Return only, with no option to destroy; deletion from inaccessible back-up systems; an unrealistically short deadline for return or destruction; a requirement to certify that every copy has been permanently erased; or no exception for information the Recipient must retain by law or under ordinary record-retention practices.

## 9. Intellectual property

Signing an NDA should not transfer ownership of either party's intellectual property or give the other party a licence to use it.

- **Preferred:** Nothing in the NDA transfers ownership of either party's pre-existing intellectual property, materials, data, developments or know-how to the other party. Neither party receives any licence or intellectual property rights except the limited right to use the other party's Confidential Information for the permitted purpose.
- **Fallback:** — (none)
- **Resist:** Any provision that transfers ownership of either party's intellectual property to the other, requires either party to assign its inventions, improvements, feedback, data or know-how, or restricts either party's use of its pre-existing or independently developed materials.

## 10. Remedies and liability

The NDA may allow a party to ask a court for an injunction if Confidential Information is misused. Check for fixed payments, indemnities and liability provisions that unfairly expose the client to liability.

- **Preferred:** Either party may seek injunctive or other equitable relief where appropriate, but the NDA should not state that the Discloser is automatically entitled to an injunction following a breach. Include an exclusion of indirect, consequential, special and punitive damages for the Recipient, to the extent permitted by applicable law. No special indemnity or liquidated damages should be imposed on the Recipient.
- **Fallback:** Accept a mutual provision allowing either party to seek injunctive or other equitable relief. Accept a mutual exclusion of indirect, consequential, special and punitive damages where commercially appropriate and consistent with the main transaction documents.
- **Resist:** Wording stating that the Discloser is automatically entitled to an injunction following a breach; an automatic waiver of any requirement to post a bond; indemnities, liquidated damages or fixed payments imposed only on the Recipient; legal fees payable only by the Recipient; a liability cap or exclusion of damages that protects only the Discloser; or an exclusion of indirect or consequential damages that applies only to the Discloser.

*Tip:* Prefer "may seek an injunction" over wording that says a party "is entitled to" an injunction.

## 11. Governing law and jurisdiction

- **Preferred:** The NDA should be governed by New York law, with exclusive jurisdiction over disputes in the state courts located in New York County, New York, or the federal courts located in the Southern District of New York. Both parties should waive their right to a jury trial in any dispute arising out of or relating to the NDA.
- **Fallback:** Accept Delaware law and jurisdiction, or the law and jurisdiction of another neutral location that is reasonably convenient for both parties. Avoid accepting the Discloser's home jurisdiction solely because it is the Discloser's preferred forum.
- **Resist:** An unfamiliar or burdensome jurisdiction with no meaningful connection to the transaction or either party; exclusive jurisdiction in the Discloser's home jurisdiction where it would be inconvenient or disadvantageous to the client; a one-sided jury trial waiver that applies only to the Recipient; or an unclear governing law or dispute-resolution forum.

## 12. Publicity and confidentiality of discussions

*(No Preferred/Fallback/Resist table — guidance only.)*

If the NDA contains a publicity or confidentiality-of-discussions clause, ensure that the obligations apply mutually and are not imposed solely on the Recipient. Neither party should be permitted to disclose the discussions or proposed transaction, or use the other party's name or logo in a public announcement, without the other party's prior written consent.

## 13. Assignment and general provisions

*(No Preferred/Fallback/Resist table — guidance only.)*

Check that assignment rights are mutual. Neither party should be able to assign the NDA without the other party's prior written consent, except for reasonable transfers to an affiliate or successor in connection with a merger, reorganisation or sale of substantially all relevant assets, provided the assignee agrees to be bound by the NDA.

Also check that either party may end discussions and terminate the NDA by written notice, while confidentiality obligations continue to apply to information already received for the agreed confidentiality period. The NDA should make clear that neither party is required to proceed with the proposed transaction. Check for standard provisions covering the entire agreement, written amendments and notices.

## 14. Restrictive covenants

Non-solicit, non-compete and standstill clauses restrict the client's business activities. They are generally not appropriate in an NDA and, if needed, should be considered separately in the main transaction agreement.

- **Preferred:** Do not include non-solicit, no-hire, non-compete or standstill restrictions in the NDA. The NDA should protect Confidential Information, not restrict the Recipient's ordinary business, hiring or investment activities.
- **Fallback:** If the Discloser insists on a restriction, limit it to what is genuinely necessary for the transaction, with a narrow scope and short duration. Prefer to negotiate it separately in the definitive transaction agreement.
- **Resist:** Broad non-compete, no-hire, standstill or non-solicit restrictions, particularly those that restrict the Recipient's ordinary business activities or apply beyond the parties involved in the transaction.

## Reviewing mutual NDAs

In a mutual NDA, both parties can disclose and receive Confidential Information. However, where our client is primarily receiving information, review the agreement from the Recipient's perspective and apply the general Recipient-side guidelines set out in this playbook.

Pay particular attention to whether:

- Our client can share the other party's Confidential Information with its affiliates, employees, consultants and professional advisers who need to know it for the permitted purpose.
- Our client is not subject to unnecessary restrictions on its ordinary business, hiring or investment activities.
- The confidentiality period and return or destruction obligations are reasonable and workable.
- Our client is not exposed to disproportionate liability, indemnities or other onerous obligations.
- The other party does not have broader rights or protections than our client under the NDA.
- Any obligations applying when our client discloses Confidential Information are reasonable and provide equivalent protection without imposing unnecessary restrictions on our client.

## Final review

Before sending the mark-up, check that all defined terms are consistent, each proposed change works with the rest of the NDA, and no amendment unintentionally creates a new obligation or removes a protection elsewhere in the agreement. Escalate material or unresolved issues to the supervising senior.
